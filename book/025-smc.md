# Sequential Monte Carlo: Object Tracking

Two kinds of inference algorithm have appeared so far. *Importance
sampling* guesses whole solutions at once, from a proposal, and weighs
each guess by how well it explains the data. *MCMC* starts from one guess
and revises it, step by step, each step a whole solution. This chapter
meets a third kind, *sequential Monte Carlo* (SMC). SMC solves a sequence
of inference problems, each a little harder than the last, and represents
each solution as a weighted collection of samples, its *particles*. The
particles of each problem are made by extending, reweighting and
resampling those of the problem before.

The sequence often comes from data that arrive over time. A *particle
filter* infers the hidden state of a process from its first observation,
then from its first two, and so on, each time extending the particles by
one step. This chapter ports Gen.jl's tutorial *Object Tracking with
Sequential Monte Carlo*: it tracks a ship from noisy bearings alone, the
"bearings-only tracking" problem of Gilks and Berzuini (2001). It writes
the model with an Unfold, which *Composition* introduced, runs a particle
filter over it, shows what resampling does and what happens without it,
and adds MCMC *rejuvenation* moves, among them one with a custom proposal.
Gen.jl's tutorial writes the model first with a loop and moves to the
Unfold for speed; the last section here makes that comparison.

## The model

We stand at the origin and watch a ship far away. We cannot see how far it
is, only its *bearing*: the angle between the positive $x$ axis and the ray
from us to the ship. We know roughly where the ship started and how fast it
was going, and we believe its velocity changes a little at random each
step. From a sequence of bearings we want its positions.

The state at time $t$ is a position $(x_t, y_t)$ and a velocity
$(v^x_t, v^y_t)$. The starting state has a normal prior around $(0.01,
0.95)$ and velocity $(0.002, -0.013)$, with standard deviation 0.01 for
each coordinate. Each step, each velocity coordinate moves by a normal
change of standard deviation $\sigma_v = 10^{-3}$, the position moves by
the new velocity, and the bearing is measured with normal noise of
standard deviation $\sigma_z = 0.005$ radians:

$$
v^x_t \sim \mathcal{N}(v^x_{t-1}, \sigma_v), \quad
x_t = x_{t-1} + v^x_t, \quad
z_t \sim \mathcal{N}(\operatorname{atan2}(y_t, x_t), \sigma_z),
$$

and the same for $y$. The bearing is `array/atan2`, Gen.jl's `atan(y, x)`:

<!-- code: constants -->
```clojure
(def measurement-noise
  "The standard deviation of a bearing, in radians."
  0.005)

(def velocity-sd
  "The standard deviation of a velocity's change in one step (Gen.jl's
   velocity_var is its square, 1e-6)."
  1e-3)

(defn bearing
  "The angle between the positive x axis and the ray from the origin to
   (x, y), between -pi and pi: Gen.jl's atan(y, x)."
  [x y]
  (array/atan2 y x))
```

One step of the ship is the kernel of an Unfold. It takes the step number,
the previous state and the parameters, makes the step's three choices, and
returns the new state, a map:

<!-- code: kernel -->
```clojure
(def move
  (gen [t {:keys [x y vx vy]} velocity-sd measurement-noise]
    (let [vx (trace :vx (d/normal vx velocity-sd))
          vy (trace :vy (d/normal vy velocity-sd))
          x (+ x vx)
          y (+ y vy)]
      (trace :z (d/normal (bearing x y) measurement-noise))
      {:x x :y y :vx vx :vy vy})))

(def moves (comb/unfold move))
```

Gen.jl's model updates its variables in place inside a `for` loop; here
the Unfold threads the state from one step to the next, and nothing is
mutated. Gen.jl's kernel receives the velocity's variance and the
measurement noise as arguments; this one receives the velocity's standard
deviation and the noise. The model draws the
starting state and its bearing, then splices the Unfold at `:moves`,
passing the parameters on. `tracker` builds the model for given
parameters, so that the workshop can try others; `model` has the true
ones:

<!-- code: model -->
```clojure
(defn tracker
  "The tracking model, a velocity changing by velocity-sd in one step and
   a bearing measured with noise measurement-noise."
  [velocity-sd measurement-noise]
  (gen [n]
    (let [x (trace :x0 (d/normal 0.01 0.01))
          y (trace :y0 (d/normal 0.95 0.01))
          vx (trace :vx0 (d/normal 0.002 0.01))
          vy (trace :vy0 (d/normal -0.013 0.01))
          start {:x x :y y :vx vx :vy vy}]
      (trace :z0 (d/normal (bearing x y) measurement-noise))
      (into [start]
            (splice :moves moves n start velocity-sd measurement-noise)))))

(def model (tracker velocity-sd measurement-noise))
```

The model's argument is the number of moves, as Gen.jl's `T` is. The
choices of time 0, the starting state and its bearing, are at `:x0`,
`:y0`, `:vx0`, `:vy0` and `:z0`. The Unfold counts its steps from 0, so
the state at time $t \geq 1$ comes from move $t - 1$, whose choices are at
`[:moves (dec t) :vx]`, `[:moves (dec t) :vy]` and `[:moves (dec t) :z]`:
Gen.jl's `(:vx, 1)` is `[:moves 0 :vx]` here. The model returns the
states, the starting one first. Two moves make eleven choices:

<!-- code: small-trace -->
```clojure
(def small (gfi/simulate model [2] (rng/key 1)))

(count (cm/addresses (:choices small)))
;=> 11

(take 4 (cm/addresses (:choices small)))
;=> [[:moves 0 :vx] [:moves 0 :vy] [:moves 0 :z] [:moves 1 :vx]]

(count (:retval small))
;=> 3
```

## The data

As in Gen.jl's tutorial, the data come from the model itself: a run of 50
moves with the starting state fixed at its prior mean. The bearings are
the run's choices at `:z0` and at each move's `:z`, read as host numbers
with `array/item`:

<!-- code: data -->

Many paths fit these bearings. On each ray the ship could be anywhere, and
a little off it, since the bearings are noisy. What rules paths out is the

change the prior makes very improbable.

## A particle filter

In gen.clj, as in Gen.jl, a particle is a trace, and the state of a
particle filter is a weighted collection of traces. The state is a map:
the particles, their unnormalized log weights at `:log-weights`, and at
`:log-ml` the part of the estimate of the log marginal likelihood that
earlier resamplings set aside. Gen.jl's operations
mutate the state; gen.clj's return a new one.

- `pf/initialize` makes `n` particles of a model on its arguments, each
  generated with the first observations. The choices the observations
  leave open come from the model's internal proposal, here the prior of the
  starting state, and each weight is that of `generate`.
- `pf/step` updates every particle's trace to new arguments with new
  observations, and adds each update's weight to the particle's log weight.
- `pf/maybe-resample` resamples the particles in proportion to their
  weights when the effective sample size falls below a threshold, half the
  number of particles by default, as Gen.jl's `maybe_resample!` does with
  `ess_threshold = n/2`.

Bearing $t$ is constrained at `:z0` for $t = 0$ and at move $t - 1$'s `:z`
after that:

<!-- code: observation -->
```clojure
(defn observation
  "The choice map of bearing t of zs: z0 at t = 0, and move t - 1's after
   that."
  [zs t]
  (if (zero? t)
    (cm/choicemap :z0 (zs 0))
    (cm/choicemap [:moves (dec t) :z] (zs t))))
```

The filter starts with the model on zero moves and the first bearing. Step
$t$ then extends every particle to $t$ moves with bearing $t$. The number
of moves changes, and its argdiff is `diff/unknown-change`, as Gen.jl's
`UnknownChange()`. Before each step a function `prepare` revises the
state: resampling here, and rejuvenation moves later in the chapter, so
that one function serves every filter of the chapter where Gen.jl copies
its filter for each. Every operation takes its own key, split from the
filter's. The filter keeps the effective sample size after each step, a
host number, rather than the states themselves:

<!-- code: particle-filter -->
```clojure
(defn particle-filter
  "A particle filter of n particles of model over the bearings zs,
   revising the state with (prepare state t key) before step t: {:state,
   the state after the last bearing, :ess, the effective sample size after
   each step}. opts go to pf/initialize."
  [model zs n prepare opts key]
  (let [[k-init & ks] (rng/split key (count zs))
        init (pf/initialize model [0] (observation zs 0) n
                            (assoc opts :key k-init))]
    (reduce (fn [{:keys [state ess]} [t k]]
              (let [[k-prepare k-step] (rng/split k)
                    state (pf/step (prepare state t k-prepare)
                                   [t] [diff/unknown-change]
                                   (observation zs t) {:key k-step})]
                {:state state :ess (conj ess (pf/ess state))}))
            {:state init :ess [(pf/ess init)]}
            (map vector (range 1 (count zs)) ks))))

(defn resample
  "Resample when the effective sample size is below half the number of
   particles."
  [state _ key]
  (pf/maybe-resample state {:key key}))
```

Gen.jl's tutorial runs 5000 particles and draws 200 unweighted traces from
the final state with `sample_unweighted_traces`; here
`pf/sample-unweighted-traces` draws 100 of them for the figure. Every
particle visits the same addresses, so with `{:batched? true}` the 5000
particles run as one batched execution: the model's code runs once per
step, on arrays of every particle's values.

<!-- code: run-filter -->
```clojure
(def filtered
  (particle-filter model zs 5000 resample {:batched? true} (rng/key 3)))

(def samples (pf/sample-unweighted-traces (:state filtered) 100 (rng/key 4)))
```

The posterior is broad, as Gen.jl's tutorial finds: many paths explain the
bearings. It is broad in one direction. Across the rays the bearings pin
the ship down; along them, its distance stays uncertain. The samples show
it at the last step. The bearing of the final position varies by about
the measurement noise, 0.005 radians, but its distance from the origin
varies by about a sixth of its mean:

<!-- code: posterior-shape -->
```clojure
(defn final-bearing
  "The bearing of trace tr's last position."
  [tr]
  (let [{:keys [x y]} (peek (:retval tr))] (array/item (bearing x y))))

(defn final-range
  "The distance from the origin of trace tr's last position."
  [tr]
  (let [{:keys [x y]} (peek (:retval tr))]
    (array/item (array/sqrt (+ (* x x) (* y y))))))

(defn spread
  "The mean and standard deviation of (f tr) over the traces trs."
  [f trs]
  (let [xs (mapv f trs)
        m (/ (reduce + xs) (count xs))]
    {:mean m
     :sd (Math/sqrt (/ (reduce + (map #(* (- % m) (- % m)) xs))
                       (dec (count xs))))}))

(def bearing-spread (spread final-bearing samples))

(def range-spread (spread final-range samples))

[bearing-spread range-spread]
;=> [{:mean 0.5233 :sd 0.0042} {:mean 0.556 :sd 0.098}] ; float64
```

At the mean distance, an uncertainty in bearing of 0.004 radians is an
uncertainty across the ray of about 0.002, and the distance along the ray
is uncertain by more than ten times that. The ship's true distance is
within five standard deviations of the posterior's mean:

<!-- code: across-the-ray -->
```clojure
(let [across (* (:mean range-spread) (:sd bearing-spread))]
  [(< (:sd bearing-spread) (* 2 measurement-noise))
   (> (:sd range-spread) (* 10 across))])
;=> [true true]

(< (abs (- (:mean range-spread) (final-range truth)))
   (* 5 (:sd range-spread)))
;=> true
```

What does one step do? Each particle's trace is extended by one move. The
new move's velocities come from the internal proposal, their prior given
the previous state, so their density cancels from the update's weight,
and the weight is the new bearing's density at the new position. On three
particles, the step's change in each log weight is that density, and the
starting choices are untouched:

<!-- code: one-step -->
```clojure
(def before (pf/initialize model [0] (observation zs 0) 3 {:key (rng/key 5)}))

(def after
  (pf/step before [1] [diff/unknown-change] (observation zs 1)
           {:key (rng/key 6)}))

(defn bearing-density
  "log p(z | tr): the density of bearing z at trace tr's last position."
  [tr z]
  (dc/log-prob (d/normal (final-bearing tr) measurement-noise) z))

(array/allclose?
 (array/array (mapv - (:log-weights after) (:log-weights before)))
 (array/array (mapv #(bearing-density % (zs 1)) (pf/traces after))))
;=> true

(defn starts
  "The starting x of each particle of a state."
  [state]
  (mapv #(array/item (% :x0)) (pf/traces state)))

(= (starts before) (starts after))
;=> true
```

A step may only add choices. An observation at an address the trace
already has, with another value, would change a choice the filter has
already weighed, and `pf/step` refuses it, as Gen.jl's
`particle_filter_step!` does:

<!-- code: step-error -->
```clojure
;; error: A particle filter step changed or removed choices
(pf/step after [1] [diff/no-change] (cm/choicemap :z0 1.5)
         {:key (rng/key 7)})
```

## Resampling

The weights record how well each particle has explained the bearings so
far. They multiply from step to step, so the particles' weights grow
apart. The *effective sample size* (ESS) of normalized weights $w_i$ is
$1/\sum_i w_i^2$: the number of particles when the weights are equal, and
1 when one particle carries all the weight. *Resampling* draws $n$
particles from the current ones in proportion to their weights, with
replacement, and gives them equal weights. Particles that explain the
bearings well are copied; those that explain them poorly are dropped, and
the filter spends its effort where the posterior is.

Gen.jl's tutorial asks what happens without it. The filter is the same,
with a `prepare` that leaves the state alone:

<!-- code: no-resampling -->
```clojure
(defn no-resampling
  "The state as it is."
  [state _ _]
  state)

(def unresampled
  (particle-filter model zs 5000 no-resampling {:batched? true} (rng/key 3)))

(peek (:ess unresampled))
;=> 1.0 ; float64

(< (peek (:ess unresampled)) 10)
;=> true
```

After the last bearing, one particle carries all the weight. Without
resampling, the filter is importance sampling of whole paths from the
prior, and after 50 bearings almost every path drawn from the prior has
long since strayed from the rays. The samples show the *particle
collapse*: a hundred draws from the final state are one path, drawn a
hundred times.

<!-- code: collapse -->
```clojure
(def unresampled-samples
  (pf/sample-unweighted-traces (:state unresampled) 100 (rng/key 4)))

(count (distinct (map final-range unresampled-samples)))
;=> 1 ; float64

(< (count (distinct (map final-range unresampled-samples))) 5)
;=> true
```

With resampling, the ESS falls at each step as a new bearing weighs the
particles, and whenever it falls below 2500 the next step begins by
resampling. Without it, the ESS falls, with small ups and downs, until
one particle carries all the weight.

## Rejuvenation

The filter guesses each move's velocities once, when it adds the move, and
never revises them. Later bearings say a lot about earlier positions, but
the filter cannot use what they say. Resampling makes it worse. It copies
the particles that explain the bearings so far and drops the others, so
after many steps the particles descend from a few early ones, and share
their early moves. This is *path degeneracy*.

A batched filter's state holds its particles as one batched collection,
under `:particles`, and `batched/choice` reads one choice of every particle
as an array. A filter of scalar traces reads them from the traces:

<!-- code: values-at -->
```clojure
(defn values-at
  "Every particle's choice at addr, as host numbers."
  [state addr]
  (if-let [ps (:particles state)]
    (array/->clj (batched/choice ps addr))
    (mapv #(array/item (% addr)) (pf/traces state))))

(defn distinct-at
  "How many distinct values the particles hold at addr."
  [state addr]
  (count (distinct (values-at state addr))))
```

The 5000 particles of the filter above hold only a few dozen distinct
starting positions:

<!-- code: degeneracy -->
```clojure
(distinct-at (:state filtered) :x0)
;=> 35 ; float64

(< (distinct-at (:state filtered) :x0) 500)
;=> true
```

MCMC moves between steps can revise old choices. These *rejuvenation*
moves (Gilks and Berzuini's term) target the current posterior: after
three bearings, a move's stationary distribution is the posterior given
those three. Such a move leaves the posterior where it is, so the weights
stay as they are. Rejuvenation can target any choices; common targets are
the global ones, which every step depends on, such as the starting state,
and a sliding window of recent ones.

Gen.jl's first version resimulates the starting choices: one MH move
on each particle, with a proposal from their prior, `mh` with a
selection. `mh/mh-particles` makes that move on every particle of a
batched collection at once, and keeps it batched. (`pf/rejuvenate`, below,
takes any MCMC kernel, and moves one particle at a time.)

<!-- code: resimulate -->
```clojure
(def initial-choices (sel/select :x0 :y0 :vx0 :vy0))

(defn resimulate-start
  "One MH move on the initial choices of every particle of a batched
   filter, proposed from their prior, then resampling as before."
  [state t key]
  (let [[k-move k-resample] (rng/split key)
        moved (mh/mh-particles (:particles state) initial-choices k-move)]
    (resample (assoc state :particles (:particles moved)) t k-resample)))

(def resimulated
  (particle-filter model zs 5000 resimulate-start {:batched? true}
                   (rng/key 8)))

(distinct-at (:state resimulated) :x0)
;=> 36 ; float64
```

The starting positions are still few. A proposal from the prior is a
blind guess, and the posterior of the starting state given many bearings
is far narrower than its prior, so the guesses are almost never accepted.
At the last step, fewer than one move in a hundred is accepted:

<!-- code: resimulate-acceptance -->
```clojure
(def final-resimulation
  (mh/mh-particles (:particles (:state resimulated)) initial-choices
                   (rng/key 9)))

(def resimulation-rate
  (/ (count (filter true? (:accepted? final-resimulation))) 5000.0))

resimulation-rate
;=> 0.0008 ; float64

(< resimulation-rate 0.01)
;=> true
```

Gen.jl's second version uses a custom proposal that looks at the current
trace: a random walk on the velocities of the last five moves. The
proposal is a generative function whose first argument is the trace, and
it makes its choices at the model's own addresses, which is how MH knows
which of the model's choices it proposes. Each velocity moves by a normal
step of standard deviation $10^{-3}$. At step $t$ the particles hold $t -
1$ moves, so the window ends at move $t - 2$:

<!-- code: perturbation -->
```clojure
(def perturbation
  (gen [tr a b]
    (doseq [t (range a (inc b))]
      (trace [:moves t :vx] (d/normal (tr [:moves t :vx]) 1e-3))
      (trace [:moves t :vy] (d/normal (tr [:moves t :vy]) 1e-3)))))

(defn recent
  "The first and last of the last five moves of a trace with n moves."
  [n]
  [(max 0 (- n 5)) (dec n)])

(defn perturb-recent
  "One random-walk MH move on the velocities of every particle's last five
   moves, then resampling as before."
  [state t key]
  (let [[k-move k-resample] (rng/split key)
        n (dec t)
        moved (if (zero? n)
                state
                (pf/rejuvenate state (kernel/mh perturbation (recent n))
                               k-move))]
    (resample moved t k-resample)))
```

`pf/rejuvenate` applies an MCMC kernel to every particle, and
`kernel/mh` makes the kernel of a proposal and its arguments. An MH move
with a custom proposal has no batched form, so `pf/rejuvenate` runs one
particle at a time and turns the batched state into one of traces. This
filter therefore runs 1000 particles, and is compared with a plain filter
of 1000:

<!-- code: perturbed -->
```clojure
(def plain
  (particle-filter model zs 1000 resample {:batched? true} (rng/key 10)))

(def perturbed
  (particle-filter model zs 1000 perturb-recent {:batched? true}
                   (rng/key 10)))
```

The random walk proposes values near the current ones, and is accepted
far more often than resimulation:

<!-- code: perturb-acceptance -->
```clojure
(def perturbation-rate
  (let [trs (pf/traces (:state perturbed))
        moved (map #(mh/mh %1 perturbation (recent T) %2)
                   trs (rng/split (rng/key 11) (count trs)))]
    (/ (count (filter :accepted? moved)) (double (count trs)))))

perturbation-rate
;=> 0.176 ; float64

(> perturbation-rate (* 10 resimulation-rate))
;=> true
```

What does it buy? Path degeneracy can be measured move by move: the
number of distinct values of a move's `:vx` among the final particles.
Every particle has its own last move, and the earlier the move, the fewer
ancestors the particles share there. An accepted rejuvenation move gives a
particle new values at its last five moves, where resampling had left
copies. In these two runs, the rejuvenated filter's particles hold more
distinct values at the moves near the end: at move 44, the oldest in the
last window, 222 against 94.

## Why an Unfold

Gen.jl's tutorial first writes the model with a loop in the body, and
rewrites it with an Unfold to make the filter faster. Here is the loop
version, with the same addresses:

<!-- code: loop-model -->
```clojure
(def loop-model
  (gen [n]
    (let [x (trace :x0 (d/normal 0.01 0.01))
          y (trace :y0 (d/normal 0.95 0.01))
          vx (trace :vx0 (d/normal 0.002 0.01))
          vy (trace :vy0 (d/normal -0.013 0.01))]
      (trace :z0 (d/normal (bearing x y) measurement-noise))
      (loop [t 0
             states [{:x x :y y :vx vx :vy vy}]]
        (if (< t n)
          (let [{:keys [x y vx vy]} (peek states)
                vx (trace [:moves t :vx] (d/normal vx velocity-sd))
                vy (trace [:moves t :vy] (d/normal vy velocity-sd))
                x (+ x vx)
                y (+ y vy)]
            (trace [:moves t :z] (d/normal (bearing x y) measurement-noise))
            (recur (inc t) (conj states {:x x :y y :vx vx :vy vy})))
          states)))))
```

It is the same model. It gives the true trace's choices the same density,
and extending it by one move, with the move's choices given, has the same
weight:

<!-- code: same-model -->
```clojure
(array/allclose? (:weight (gfi/assess loop-model [T] (:choices truth)))
                 (:score truth))
;=> true

(def loop-truth
  (:trace (gfi/generate loop-model [T] (:choices truth) (rng/key 12))))

(def next-move
  (cm/choicemap [:moves T :vx] 0.004 [:moves T :vy] -0.012
                [:moves T :z] 0.5))

(defn extend-by-one
  "Trace tr of n moves updated to n + 1, with the choices cs."
  [tr cs]
  (gfi/update tr [(inc (first (:args tr)))] [diff/unknown-change] cs
              (rng/key 13)))

(array/allclose? (:weight (extend-by-one truth next-move))
                 (:weight (extend-by-one loop-truth next-move)))
;=> true
```

The difference is in the work. Updating a `gen` function's trace runs its
body again, every step of the loop, so the filter's step $t$ costs time
proportional to $t$, and the whole filter time quadratic in the number of
bearings. An Unfold keeps each move's trace, and an update that adds a
move revisits only the moves whose inputs may have changed. Extending the
true trace revisits move 0 alone:

<!-- code: revisited -->
```clojure
(defn revisited
  "The moves whose subtraces differ between the traces a and b of model."
  [a b]
  (vec (remove #(identical? (trace/subtrace a [:moves %])
                            (trace/subtrace b [:moves %]))
               (range (first (:args a))))))

(revisited truth (:trace (extend-by-one truth next-move)))
;=> [0]
```

Move 0 is revisited because its input, the starting state, is a new map
each time the model's body runs, and `update` compares arguments by
identity. It returns the same state as before, so the Unfold revisits
nothing after it. (The static modeling language of *Speeding Up* tracks
such changes through the body, and revisits nothing.) A step of the
filter therefore costs the same at every $t$.

Gen.jl's tutorial times both filters for up to 500 bearings and finds the
loop's time quadratic, the Unfold's linear. Timings vary from machine to
machine, so the check here is conservative: the median ratio of the two
models' times to extend a trace by one move, over 101 tries. It grows
with the number of moves, and at 200 moves the loop takes more than five
times as long:

<!-- code: timing -->
```clojure
(defn millis
  "How long (f) takes to run, in milliseconds."
  [f]
  (let [start (System/nanoTime)]
    (f)
    (/ (- (System/nanoTime) start) 1e6)))

(defn cost-ratio
  "The median, over reps runs, of the time the loop model takes to extend a
   trace of n moves by one, over the time the Unfold takes."
  [n reps]
  (let [tr (gfi/simulate model [n] (rng/key 14))
        loop-tr (:trace (gfi/generate loop-model [n] (:choices tr)
                                      (rng/key 14)))
        bearing-n (cm/choicemap [:moves n :z] 0.5)
        ratio #(/ (millis (fn [] (extend-by-one loop-tr bearing-n)))
                  (millis (fn [] (extend-by-one tr bearing-n))))]
    (nth (sort (repeatedly reps ratio)) (quot reps 2))))

(def cost-50 (cost-ratio 50 101))

(def cost-200 (cost-ratio 200 101))

[(> cost-200 5) (> cost-200 cost-50)]
;=> [true true]
```

The particle filter here is the basic loop: propagate, weigh, resample,
and optionally rejuvenate. *SMC in Depth* takes it further: a Kalman
filter's exact answer to check a particle filter against, the resampling
schemes, degeneracy, SMCP3's proposals, and particle filters inside MCMC.

## Workshop

Each exercise has a tested solution at the end of the chapter.

1. **Fewer particles.** A filter's state also estimates the log marginal
   likelihood of the bearings, $\log p(z_0, \dots, z_{50})$, with
   `pf/log-ml-estimate`. Run 30 filters of 50 particles and 10 of 800,
   each with its own key. Predict how the two sets of estimates differ,
   then check.
2. **Which motion model?** The bearings were made with $\sigma_v =
   10^{-3}$. Build the model with $\sigma_v = 10^{-4}$, a ship that holds
   its course, and with $10^{-2}$, one that wanders, using `tracker`.
   Which model gives the bearings the highest marginal likelihood? Run
   five filters of 500 particles for each.
3. **A random walk on the start.** Resimulating the starting state is
   rarely accepted. Write a proposal that moves each of the four starting
   choices by a normal step of standard deviation 0.001, and compare how
   often each move is accepted on the final particles of the rejuvenated
   filter.

## Solutions

**1.** With 800 particles, the estimates agree with each other, with a
standard deviation of about 0.6:

<!-- code: solution-1 -->
```clojure
(defn log-ml
  "The estimate of log p(zs) of a filter of n particles of model."
  [model n key]
  (pf/log-ml-estimate
   (:state (particle-filter model zs n resample {:batched? true} key))))

(def few (mapv #(log-ml model 50 %) (rng/split (rng/key 20) 30)))

(def many (mapv #(log-ml model 800 %) (rng/split (rng/key 21) 10)))

(spread identity many)
;=> {:mean 172.05 :sd 0.65} ; float64

(count (filter #(< % (- (:mean (spread identity many)) 10)) few))
;=> 10 ; float64
```

With 50, most estimates land near them, but a third fall more than 10
below their mean. When all
50 particles' predicted bearings miss an observed bearing by many times
its noise, every weight is tiny: the filter has lost the ship, and the
estimate says the bearings are very improbable. Every estimate with 800
particles is within five standard deviations of their mean; some with 50
are below it by more:

<!-- code: solution-1-check -->
```clojure
(let [{:keys [mean sd]} (spread identity many)]
  [(every? #(< (abs (- % mean)) (* 5 sd)) many)
   (pos? (count (filter #(< % (- mean (* 5 sd))) few)))])
;=> [true true]
```

**2.** Each filter's estimate of $\log p(z)$ is the evidence for its model,
and the true one wins:

<!-- code: solution-2 -->
```clojure
(def steadiness
  "The estimates of log p(zs) of five filters under each velocity-sd."
  (into {}
        (for [v [1e-4 1e-3 1e-2]]
          [v (mapv #(log-ml (tracker v measurement-noise) 500 %)
                   (rng/split (rng/key 22) 5))])))

(defn mean-and-se
  "The mean of xs and its standard error."
  [xs]
  (let [{:keys [mean sd]} (spread identity xs)]
    {:mean mean :se (/ sd (Math/sqrt (count xs)))}))

(update-vals steadiness #(:mean (mean-and-se %)))
;=> {1.0E-4 -7964.3 0.001 171.98 0.01 159.36} ; float64
```

The wandering ship spreads its prior over many paths the bearings rule
out, so it gives the bearings less probability: it loses by about 13 nats,
far more than five standard errors of the difference. The steady ship
cannot turn as the true one did. Its filters lose track, and every one of
its estimates falls below every estimate of the true model:

<!-- code: solution-2-check -->
```clojure
(defn exceeds?
  "Does the estimate a exceed the estimate b by more than five standard
   errors of their difference?"
  [a b]
  (> (- (:mean a) (:mean b))
     (* 5 (Math/sqrt (+ (Math/pow (:se a) 2) (Math/pow (:se b) 2))))))

[(exceeds? (mean-and-se (steadiness 1e-3)) (mean-and-se (steadiness 1e-2)))
 (< (apply max (steadiness 1e-4)) (apply min (steadiness 1e-3)))]
;=> [true true]
```

**3.** The proposal looks at the trace, as the perturbation does. The
same final particles take both moves:

<!-- code: solution-3 -->
```clojure
(def walk-start
  (gen [tr]
    (doseq [a [:x0 :y0 :vx0 :vy0]]
      (trace a (d/normal (tr a) 0.001)))))

(defn acceptance
  "The fraction of the traces trs that the MH move (move tr key) moves."
  [move trs key]
  (/ (count (filter :accepted? (map move trs (rng/split key (count trs)))))
     (double (count trs))))

(def final-traces (pf/traces (:state perturbed)))

(def resimulation-acceptance
  (acceptance #(mh/mh %1 initial-choices %2) final-traces (rng/key 23)))

(def walk-acceptance
  (acceptance #(mh/mh %1 walk-start [] %2) final-traces (rng/key 23)))

[resimulation-acceptance walk-acceptance]
;=> [0.003 0.329] ; float64
```

The random walk proposes starting states near ones that already explain
the bearings, so it is accepted about a hundred times as often, a
difference of far more than five standard errors:

<!-- code: solution-3-check -->
```clojure
(let [n (count final-traces)
      variance #(/ (* % (- 1 %)) n)]
  (> (- walk-acceptance resimulation-acceptance)
     (* 5 (Math/sqrt (+ (variance walk-acceptance)
                        (variance resimulation-acceptance))))))
;=> true
```
